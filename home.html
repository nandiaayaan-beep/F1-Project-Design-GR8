Introduction to the Sport


Formula 1, often shortened to F1, is the highest level of single-seater racing sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA). Across a season of Grands Prix, the world's fastest drivers and most advanced racing teams compete for two titles: the Drivers' Championship and the Constructors' Championship. What makes F1 special is the mix of human skill and engineering detail: race strategy, tire management, aerodynamics, power units, pit stops, and split-second decision-making all matter. The video highlights the final lap of the 2021 Abu Dhabi Grand Prix, one of the most dramatic championship finishes in modern F1, when Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton fought for the title until the last lap of the final race.

Formula 1 cars regularly exceed 300 km/h, but their real advantage is how quickly they accelerate, brake, and change direction.

Aerodynamics are the defining feature of an F1 car. Wings, floors, sidepods, and diffusers create downforce so the car can grip the track at extreme speed.

Formula 1 drivers can experience forces above 5 G in heavy braking and fast corners, so neck strength, core fitness, and concentration are essential.

The first Formula 1 World Championship race was held in 1950 at Silverstone, and the sport has grown into a global championship watched by millions.
