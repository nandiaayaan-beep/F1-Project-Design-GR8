Introduction to the Sport


Formula 1, also known as F1, is the highest class of single-seater auto racing sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA). The sport features a series of high speed and high intensity races that take place on circuits around the world, with teams and drivers competing for the World Championship title. F1 cars are known for their advanced technology, aerodynamics, and speed, making it one of the most exciting and prestigious motorsports in the world. Here is a video of one of the most iconic moments in F1 history, being the final lap of the 2021 Abu Dhabi Grand Prix, in which Max Verstrappen raced against Lewis Hamilton for one of the most intense world championship titles in the history of the sport, with the title being decided in the final lap of the race.

Formula 1 cars can reach speeds of over 300kmph, with the highest recorded speed being 372.6 kmph.

The key defining factors of F1 cars are infact their aerodynamics, not their engines, with multiple other cars featuring horsepower's significantly higher than F1 cars, with better V8 and V10 turbocahrged engines.

Formula 1 drivers can face G-forces of up to 5 Gs during high speed corners. Drivers of this sport have to train their neck muscles to withstand these superhuman forces!

The first Formula 1 World Championship was held in 1950, and the sport has since evolved into a global phenomenon with millions of fans worldwide.
